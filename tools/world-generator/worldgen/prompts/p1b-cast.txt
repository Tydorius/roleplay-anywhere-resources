- Give EVERY building a status. "home": a household lives here. "staffed": open,
  with people working it. "occasional": a visitor works it now and then.
  "vacant": closed but sound, waiting for someone. "abandoned": left to rot.
- Not every building is in use. Choose what the world can really keep running.
  A smithy with no smith, a shop whose owner died and an estate gone to seed are
  story hooks, not gaps: mark them, and write a "why" worth a quest.
- Everyone who lives here belongs to a household, and a staffed building's
  workers are household members whose "work" names it. A staffed building needs
  at least one. Nobody lives or works in an occasional, vacant or abandoned one.
- A building can be both: an inn's keeper may live in, so the inn is "staffed"
  and has a household. A unit building holds several households, each in a
  flat of its own: code sets how many flats it has and how many beds each from
  your households, so ignore the copies its line lists. At most 8 to a household.
- Families, not just workers: at least a third of the homes hold children or
  elders. Children and elders have no role or work; they stay home by day.
- "couples" counts the pairs among a household's adults, at most half of them.
- "shift" is "day" unless the work is really done later: a tavern's evening
  staff, a night watch. "sells" is true when customers buy from this person,
  and false otherwise, however the role sounds: a guard in a looted shop sells
  nothing.
