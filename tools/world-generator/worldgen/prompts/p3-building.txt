Design ONE building's interior for the world below. Code splits the footprint,
adds the doors and places every object you list; you never give coordinates.

SETTING: ${setting_line}
ZONE: ${zone_line}
BUILDING: ${building_line}
COMPOUND: ${compound_line}
UNITS: ${units_line}

CATALOGUE (name | size in cells | placement rule):
${catalogue_lines}

LOOT POOLS (name: the items each holds) — empty when the catalogue has none:
${pool_lines}

Reply with ONE JSON object with exactly these fields:
{
  "name": "${building_name}",
  "wall": "wood|brick|stone|steel",
  "ground": "floor material, one or two words",
  "lootPool": "optional: a pool name covering the whole building's containers",
  "rooms": [{"name": "...", "share": "small|medium|large", "purpose": "...",
             "lootPool": "optional: a pool name covering every container in this room"}],
  "connections": [["Room A", "Room B"]],
  "entrance": {"room": "...", "side": "north|south|east|west"},
  "furniture": [{"template": "catalogue name", "room": "room name", "count": 1,
                 "hint": "...", "lootPool": "optional: a pool name for this piece"}],
  "unit": null,
  "yard": null or {"props": [{"template": "catalogue name", "count": 1, "hint": "outdoor hint"}]}
}

- A BUILDING line ending in [brackets] says what state it's in: design it that
  way, in the rooms' purposes and the furniture you choose.
- 1 to 4 rooms (one for a hut). "connections" names rooms that share an inner
  door; the entrance room must be one of "rooms".
- The LOOT POOLS list names the pools the catalogue's containers draw from.
  Point the most specific level at a pool: a furniture line's "lootPool" for
  one shelf, a room's for every container in it, the building's for all of
  them — the most specific wins, so the same shelf template can hold clothes
  in one shop and baseball bats in the next. Leave them out when the list is
  empty or nothing needs one.
- The UNITS line says when this building repeats one unit many times (the
  count and the residents per copy are there): reply with the building's
  SHARED rooms in "rooms" (the entrance hall, corridor rooms, a reception or
  common room, whatever it needs) and design ONE copy in "unit" — {"rooms", "connections", "entrance",
  "furniture"} like a tiny building (its rooms and furniture lines may carry
  "lootPool" like any other). Code stamps every copy and gives each
  its own door; give the unit one bed per resident. When a building has many
  rooms of the same kind (apartments, classrooms, hospital rooms, hotel
  rooms, cells, cabins), design ONE of them and give a count in the plan —
  never list or design the copies one by one.
- The COMPOUND line says when the building is one part of a connected
  structure (its slot and the walls, corridor or hosts beside it): its
  entrance and inner doors must make sense against those neighbours — a gate
  part is walked through, a row part's entrance faces the frontage, a spine
  part opens onto the corridor, an attached part shares one wall with its host.
- Furnish every room for its purpose, with counts that fit it and whatever
  this setting's rooms hold: a bedroom has one bed per sleeper plus storage
  and a light source; a common room has several tables with seats; a workshop
  has its tools, materials and somewhere to sit; a shop has a counter and
  shelves; a sickbay or lab has beds and equipment. Guest rooms and wards may
  hold more beds than the building has residents: those stay free for
  travellers and patients. When the BUILDING line
  says couples, each couple shares one double bed (2x2, sleeps two) in a
  bedroom of its own, with children and everyone else in other rooms when the
  building has them.
- "template" must be a catalogue name EXACTLY as listed. "room" must be one of
  your own room names.
- Furniture hints use ONLY the room vocabulary (wall:north, corner, center,
  row:east, beside:<template>, around:<template>, near_door, away_from_door,
  symmetric, joined with +). Put a hearth or stove against a wall away from
  the door, seats around tables, storage in corners.
- "name" must be exactly "${building_name}". Use "yard" only when the building
  has outside space (its plan entry has a yard); yard props use the OUTDOOR
  hint vocabulary (scatter, edge, behind:<building>, beside:<building>...).
