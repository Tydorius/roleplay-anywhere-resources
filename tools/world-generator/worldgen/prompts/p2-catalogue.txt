- Cover everything the plan implies: furniture for every room purpose in every
  building, props for every feature in each zone's "outdoor" list, and filler
  for the wild ground (trees, bushes, rubble - whatever fits the setting).
- 12 to 30 templates. One template per kind of thing, shared by all rooms.
- A container template may name its default pool from the list above:
  "lootPool": "Kitchen cupboard" on the template. P3 later points a specific
  room or shelf at a pool, so the same shelf template can hold clothes in one shop and baseball
  bats in the next.
- Sizes in cells: bed 1x2 (sleeps 1), double bed 2x2 (sleeps 2), table 2x1
  or 2x2, chair/stool/lamp 1x1, tree 1x1 to 3x3. Walkability 0 unless a
  person could step over or onto it; sleep objects are the exception — a bed,
  cot, bunk, pallet or hammock gets walkability 0.3, because sleepers lie on
  it and walking across one is slow.
- "forSleep" is true only for things a person sleeps in or on: beds, cots,
  bunks, pallets, hammocks. Every other template is false. Every home needs
  at least one forSleep template in its furniture. A sleep object at least
  2 cells wide both ways (a double bed) sleeps two; anything narrower sleeps
  one.
- "light" is 0 for anything that gives off no light. A light source gets 0.2
  to 1.0 by how far its light reaches: a candle about 0.2, a lantern or
  streetlight 0.5 to 0.7, a hearth or bonfire 0.8 to 1.0. Tag it "light". The
  game lights the map at night only from these, so homes, inns and streets
  each need some.
- Every "rule" must be concrete and setting-specific. In a medieval village:
  wells are private behind a home, or one large well in the village centre. In
  a city: no wells, but AC units against outside walls. "Somewhere suitable"
  is a failure.
