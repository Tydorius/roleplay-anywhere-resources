Slots by shape (omit "at" for a row part to take the plan's order):
- enclosure: corner:<nw|ne|sw|se>, edge:<north|south|east|west>:<start|center|end> (at most two
  of them "gate": true, walked through on both faces), inside:<center|north|south|east|west>,
  attached:<part>:<north|south|east|west>. The same shape reads many ways: a castle (four
  towers, a gatehouse, a keep inside) or a walled monastery (a chapel and
  cloister inside, a gatehouse and two corner lodges round them).
- row: row:<index> lines parts up sharing the frontage, their entrances on it;
  "linked": true butts a part against its neighbour with a door between them
  (a terrace, a ship's compartments), unlinked parts keep a clear gap.
  "front" puts a street, walkway or parked-vehicle strip in front.
- spine: spine:<left|right>:<index> along the corridor, spine:end:<start|end>
  capping it, spine:hub for the one hub part whose outward entrance gates the
  block. A cluster is the spineWidth-0 case: no corridor, parts packed round
  the hub.
- any shape: attached:<part>:<north|south|east|west> branches a wing off another part.

These show the vocabulary, not templates. Combine shapes freely to fit the idea.

- "compounds" is optional: omit the field when no place needs one. Use a
  compound when ONE place is built from several connected structures, and
  name its parts as ordinary buildings of that zone first.
- Most buildings are not in a compound.
- When a building has many rooms of the same kind (apartments, classrooms,
  hospital rooms, hotel rooms, cells, cabins), design ONE of them and give a
  count. Code copies it. Never list or design the copies one by one. The same
  for identical neighbouring buildings: design one, and point the rest at it
  with "sameAs".
- Buildings are lived in and worked in: homes, workplaces, gathering places,
  whatever this setting has. A medieval village wants an inn, homes, a
  workshop or two, a chapel or mill; a modern town wants flats over a shop
  row, a clinic, a cafe, a depot; a station wants a mess hall, a medical bay,
  cabins, workshops. Cover the idea's named features in zones or buildings.
- The brief may name how many residents to house. Plan enough homes and
  lodgings (houses, barracks, dormitories, inn rooms) to bed them all: a home
  building may carry "residents": n (how many sleep there; never on a unit
  building, which houses copies x units.residents) and, beside it, "couples":
  n (how many pairs of its residents share a double bed; at most half the
  residents, and never on a unit building). Families usually have one couple;
  an inn's or a manor's household may have more. Few separate homes beat
  one crowded hall: families, lodgers, staff quarters.
- A zone's "arrangement", when set: "around_green" puts its buildings facing
  one open green (the largest opposite the road), "along_street" lines them
  on a street, "scattered" spreads them loosely, "fields" fills the zone
  with fenced crop plots and gathers its buildings at the road-side corner.
- 2 to 4 zones, each with a distinct kind. Every name in "adjacentTo" and in
  "roads" must be a zone in this same reply. The building cap follows the
  population the brief names (12 for a small place, up to 28 for a full one;
  4 more when the reply uses compounds — towers are buildings); a validation
  error will name the exact number if you go over.
- Choose "density" honestly (a hamlet is sparse, a town is normal, a city
  quarter is dense); it scales how much filler code spreads outdoors.
- A "yard" is null for most buildings; omit it (null) unless the building
  truly needs outside space.
