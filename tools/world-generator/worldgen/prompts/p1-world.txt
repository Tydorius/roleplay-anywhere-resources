- "compounds" is optional: omit the field when no place needs one. Use a
  compound when ONE place is built from several connected structures — four
  towers joined by walls with a keep inside — and name its parts as ordinary
  buildings of that zone first. Most buildings are not in a compound.
- Compound slots: corner parts sit on the enclosure's four corners
  (corner:nw ... corner:se), edge parts sit in a wall (edge:<side>:<start|
  center|end>, at most two of them marked "gate": true — the gate gets doors
  through it on both faces), inside parts stand in the courtyard
  (inside:<center or a side>), and an attached part shares a wall with
  another part (attached:<that part's building>:<side>).
- The same shape reads many ways: a castle (four towers, a gatehouse, a keep
  inside) or a walled monastery (a chapel and cloister inside, a gatehouse
  and two corner lodges round them). Do not take either as a template.
- A zone's "arrangement", when set: "around_green" puts its buildings facing
  one open green (the largest opposite the road), "along_street" lines them
  on a street, "scattered" spreads them loosely, "fields" fills the zone
  with fenced crop plots and gathers its buildings at the road-side corner.
- 2 to 4 zones, each with a distinct kind. Every name in "adjacentTo" and in
  "roads" must be a zone in this same reply. No more than 12 buildings in
  the whole plan (16 when the reply uses compounds — towers are buildings).
- Buildings are lived in: an inn, homes, a workshop or two, a chapel or mill if
  the idea asks for one. A "yard" is null for most buildings; omit it (null)
  unless the building truly needs outside space.
- The world must work with the idea: cover its named features (inn, mill,
  chapel, ...) in zones or buildings.
- Choose "density" honestly (a hamlet is sparse, a town is normal, a city
  quarter is dense); it scales how much filler code spreads outdoors.
